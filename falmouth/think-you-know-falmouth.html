Think You Know Falmouth?

From a town settled in 1660 to a Woods Hole team that helped find the Titanic — stories on the free pull-out wellness map.

Open it in the flipbook → Open the pull-out map All Falmouth Stories

Think you know Falmouth? Open the fold-out map in the middle of the January 2027 issue and put a finger on a square. Letters A through F run across; numbers 1 through 4 run down. The front is Downtown and Main Street. The back is Main Street and Woods Hole. Every story here is a real, checked place, and each one ends with a grid square.

A town since 1686

The Massachusetts Secretary of the Commonwealth records that English colonists settled Falmouth in 1660 and that the town was incorporated in 1686. The Village Green, where Main Street meets Palmer Avenue and Town Hall Square, is still the civic heart of the village. First Congregational Church, gathered in 1708 and moved to 68 Main Street in 1857, looks over the Green; a Paul Revere bell in its steeple still rings. Just up Palmer Avenue, Falmouth Museums on the Green keep the public record, including the c. 1790 Dr. Francis Wicks House and the c. 1730 Conant House.

Find it Downtown side A1 (the Green; also drawn on the Woods Hole side at C2). Town Hall is Downtown B2. The museums are Downtown B1.

From sea to shining sea

Katharine Lee Bates, who wrote “America the Beautiful,” was born on August 12, 1859, at 16 Main Street, in the Congregational parsonage. The 1810 house is a private residence today; please view it from the sidewalk. Bates wrote the poem after a visit to Pikes Peak in 1893, taught at Wellesley College, and is buried in Oak Grove Cemetery in Falmouth. A bronze statue by Lloyd Lillie, portraying her at 34 at Pikes Peak, stands on the lawn of Falmouth Public Library at 300 Main Street. It was dedicated on August 12, 1988, during the town’s tricentennial.

Her famous line named the Shining Sea Bikeway, a 10.7-mile paved path on the old railroad bed that carried trains between Falmouth and Woods Hole from 1872 to 1965. The town dedicated the first stretch in 1975 and extended the trail to North Falmouth in 2009. It is the only bikeway on Cape Cod that runs alongside the sea.

Find it The birthplace is Downtown side B1 (private). The statue is Downtown C2. The downtown bikeway access is Downtown A2; the Woods Hole stretch is Woods Hole side B3.

Her famous line, “from sea to shining sea,” gave the bikeway its name.

Science, and a wreck in the dark

Woods Hole is one of the country’s major marine-science centers. WHOI, the Marine Biological Laboratory, and NOAA’s Woods Hole Science Aquarium share the village waterfront. Spencer Baird established a U.S. Fish Commission station here in 1871; a public aquarium was added in 1875, and NOAA calls it the nation’s oldest public marine aquarium, at 166 Water Street. On September 1, 1985, a WHOI–IFREMER expedition led by Dr. Robert D. Ballard and Jean-Louis Michel found the wreck of RMS Titanic. The aquarium is closed through early 2027 for foundation repairs.

Find it Woods Hole side A3 (the aquarium). WHOI, the Marine Biological Laboratory, and Eel Pond are in A4.

A light on the sound

Nobska Light stands at 233 Nobska Road in Woods Hole, where Buzzards Bay, Vineyard Sound, and Nantucket Sound meet. The light station was established in 1828; the present iron tower, with a brick lining, was built in 1876, automated in 1985, and listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1987. The Town of Falmouth and the Friends of Nobska Light care for the station.

Find it Woods Hole side B4.

Seven miles, every August

The Falmouth Road Race began on August 15, 1973, organized as a birthday run from the Cap’n Kidd on Water Street to Falmouth Heights. The seven-mile course runs over the Eel Pond drawbridge, past Nobska Light, along Surf Drive, around the Inner Harbor, and up into the Heights. Today runners finish at the ballfield in the Heights.

Find it The start is Woods Hole side A3. The finish is Downtown side E3.

A neighborhood you reach by boat, and one you reach by plane

In Great Harbor, off Woods Hole, a small summer community of house floats is moored near Ram Island — homes you can reach only by boat. It is a private community; view it from the public waterfront or the water, and do not approach the floats. In East Falmouth, Falmouth Airpark is a residential fly-in neighborhood, where a hangar can stand in for a garage. It is also private, and it sits off the illustrated pull-out sheet.

Find it The house floats are Woods Hole side A4, viewed from shore only. The Airpark is off the pull-out map, in East Falmouth.

The church that strawberries built

Immigrants from the Azores, Portugal, and Cape Verde turned the sandy soil of East Falmouth into a major strawberry district. Growers set aside money from berries picked on a peak Sunday of the harvest to buy land for a church where Mass could be said in Portuguese. St. Anthony’s Parish was formed in 1921, and the church at 167 East Falmouth Highway opened on January 1, 1924. It became known as “the church that strawberries built.”

Find it Off the pull-out map — East Falmouth.
