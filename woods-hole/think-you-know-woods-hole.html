A village of science

Woods Hole is a village of Falmouth, and one of the country’s major marine-science centers. The Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution (WHOI), the Marine Biological Laboratory, and NOAA’s Woods Hole Science Aquarium sit along the village waterfront. Spencer Baird established a U.S. Fish Commission research station here in 1871; a public research aquarium was added in 1875, and NOAA calls it the nation’s oldest public marine aquarium, at 166 Water Street.

On September 1, 1985, a joint expedition of WHOI and France’s IFREMER, with WHOI’s Dr. Robert D. Ballard and IFREMER’s Jean-Louis Michel, found the wreck of RMS Titanic. After the French ship Le Suroît narrowed the search, WHOI’s research vessel Knorr, using the deep-towed imaging system Argo, followed a debris trail and identified a Titanic boiler. The Knorr returned to Woods Hole on September 9, 1985, to a waterfront welcome.

The aquarium is the public doorway to this story — and as of November 2026 it is closed through early 2027 for foundation repairs and exhibit work. Check NOAA’s visit page for the reopening date. WHOI and the Marine Biological Laboratory are working research campuses; enjoy the village waterfront and official visitor information rather than walking into labs.

Find it Woods Hole side A3 (the aquarium). WHOI, the Marine Biological Laboratory, and Eel Pond are in A4, just along the waterfront.

A light on the sound

Nobska Light stands at the southwestern tip of Cape Cod in Woods Hole, at 233 Nobska Road, where Buzzards Bay, Vineyard Sound, and Nantucket Sound meet, with Martha’s Vineyard and Nonamesset Island in the view. The light station was established in 1828; the present iron tower, with a brick lining, was built in 1876. It was automated in 1985 and listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1987. The Town of Falmouth and the Friends of Nobska Light care for the station; volunteer-led tours and a museum shop run seasonally. Check Friends of Nobska Light for current hours before you go.

Find it Woods Hole side B4.

Seven miles, starting on Water Street

The Falmouth Road Race began on August 15, 1973, the 40th birthday of Woods Hole bartender Tommy Leonard. He organized a run from the Cap’n Kidd on Water Street in Woods Hole to Falmouth Heights, first as a fund-raiser, and the course has been a fixture of the running calendar ever since. The route is about seven miles: from Water Street over the Eel Pond drawbridge, past Nobska Light, along Surf Drive, around Falmouth Inner Harbor, and up into the Heights. Today runners finish at the ballfield in Falmouth Heights, between Central Park Avenue and the Falmouth Road Race Finish Line Garden. The race is held in August; check falmouthroadrace.com for the current date and course details.

Find it The start is Woods Hole side A3, on Water Street next to Eel Pond and the drawbridge in A4. The finish is Downtown side E3.

A neighborhood you reach only by boat

In Great Harbor, just off the illustrated Woods Hole shore, a handful of house floats — cottages on pontoons, often called houseboats, though they have no engine of their own — form a small summer community. Town regulations allow them to be moored within 600 linear feet of Ram Island or Devil’s Foot Island in Great Harbor from mid-April to mid-November, and in Eel Pond for winter storage. The Falmouth Select Board and local reporting have described about 14 to 19 floats, and have called this the only community of its kind on Cape Cod. They can be seen from the Woods Hole waterfront. They are private seasonal homes, not a public landing. Look from the public shore; do not board, land on, or approach the floats.

Find it Woods Hole side A4, viewed from shore only, toward Ram Island and Devil’s Foot Island.

From sea to shining sea

The Shining Sea Bikeway is a 10.7-mile paved path on the old New York, New Haven and Hartford Railroad bed, which carried trains between Falmouth and Woods Hole from 1872 to 1965. The town dedicated the first stretch as a bicentennial project in 1975 and extended the trail to North Falmouth in 2009. The name honors Katharine Lee Bates, who was born in Falmouth, and the line “from sea to shining sea.” It is the only bikeway on Cape Cod that runs alongside the sea. The Woods Hole end is on this side of the map, along the stretch from Surf Drive into the village.

Find it Woods Hole side B3. Downtown access is on the front of the map at Downtown side A2.

The ferry at the foot of the village

The Steamship Authority’s Woods Hole Terminal is at 1 Cowdry Road, on the village waterfront. Ferries run between Woods Hole and Martha’s Vineyard; the crossing is about 45 minutes, according to the Steamship Authority. There is no dockside parking at the Woods Hole terminal. Travelers park in off-site lots in Falmouth, including the Palmer Avenue Lot at 286 Palmer Avenue, and take a free Steamship Authority shuttle to the terminal. Lot status, rates, and shuttle times change, so check the Steamship Authority before you travel, or call the Woods Hole parking information line at (508) 457-7275. The terminal number is (508) 548-3788.

Find it The terminal is Woods Hole side A4. The Palmer Avenue lot is Woods Hole side C1.
